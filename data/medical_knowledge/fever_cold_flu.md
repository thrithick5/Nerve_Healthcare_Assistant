# Fever, Cold, and Influenza (Flu)

## Fever

### What is Fever?
Fever is an increase in the body's temperature, which is regulated by the hypothalamus. A normal body temperature is approximately 98.6°F (37°C). A fever is generally considered to be a temperature of 100.4°F (38°C) or higher.

### Causes of Fever
- Infections (bacterial, viral, fungal)
- Inflammatory conditions
- Medications
- Heat stroke
- Certain cancers
- Autoimmune disorders

### When to See a Doctor
- Infants under 3 months: any fever over 100.4°F (38°C)
- Children 3 months to 3 years: fever of 102.2°F (39°C) or higher
- Adults: fever above 103°F (39.4°C) or fever lasting more than 3 days
- Anyone with fever accompanied by severe headache, stiff neck, confusion, or rash

### Fever Management
- Stay hydrated by drinking plenty of fluids
- Rest adequately
- Dress in lightweight clothing
- Take acetaminophen or ibuprofen (as directed)
- Lukewarm sponge bath if temperature is very high
- Monitor temperature regularly

## Common Cold

### What is a Cold?
The common cold is a viral infection of your nose and throat (upper respiratory tract). It is usually harmless, although it might not feel that way. Many types of viruses can cause a cold, but rhinoviruses are the most common.

### Symptoms
- Runny or stuffy nose
- Sore throat
- Cough
- Congestion
- Slight body aches
- Sneezing
- Low-grade fever
- Generally feeling unwell (malaise)

### Duration
- Onset: 1-3 days after exposure
- Duration: 7-10 days
- Cough may persist for 2 weeks

### Treatment
- Rest
- Stay hydrated
- Saline nasal drops or spray
- Warm liquids (tea, broth)
- Honey (for adults and children over 1 year)
- Over-the-counter cold medications (for adults)
- Gargle with warm salt water for sore throat

## Influenza (Flu)

### What is the Flu?
Influenza (flu) is a contagious respiratory illness caused by influenza viruses. It can cause mild to severe illness, and at times can lead to death. Flu is different from a cold.

### Flu vs Cold
The flu and the common cold have similar symptoms, but the flu is generally more severe:
- Flu: sudden onset, high fever (100-104°F), severe body aches, extreme fatigue
- Cold: gradual onset, low or no fever, mild aches, mild fatigue

### Flu Symptoms
- Fever (usually high)
- Severe body aches
- Headache
- Fatigue (can last 2-3 weeks)
- Dry cough
- Sore throat
- Nasal congestion
- Chills
- Sweating

### When to Seek Emergency Care
- Difficulty breathing or shortness of breath
- Persistent pain or pressure in chest
- Sudden dizziness or confusion
- Severe or persistent vomiting
- Symptoms that improve but then return with fever and worse cough

### Flu Prevention
- Annual flu vaccination (best protection)
- Frequent hand washing
- Avoid close contact with sick people
- Cover mouth and nose when coughing or sneezing
- Disinfect frequently touched surfaces
- Stay home when sick

### Flu Treatment
- Antiviral medications (oseltamivir, zanamivir) if started within 48 hours
- Rest
- Fluids
- Over-the-counter medications for fever and symptoms
