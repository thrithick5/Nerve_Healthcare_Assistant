# Diabetes Mellitus - Overview

## What is Diabetes?

Diabetes mellitus is a chronic metabolic disorder characterized by elevated levels of blood glucose (or blood sugar), which leads over time to serious damage to the heart, blood vessels, eyes, kidneys, and nerves. The most common type is type 2 diabetes, usually in adults, which occurs when the body becomes resistant to insulin or doesn't make enough insulin. In the past three decades the prevalence of type 2 diabetes has risen dramatically in countries of all income levels.

## Types of Diabetes

### Type 1 Diabetes
Type 1 diabetes (formerly known as insulin-dependent or childhood-onset diabetes) is characterized by a lack of insulin production and is caused by the autoimmune destruction of insulin-producing beta cells in the pancreas. The cause of type 1 diabetes is not known and it is not preventable with current knowledge.

### Type 2 Diabetes
Type 2 diabetes (formerly called non-insulin-dependent or adult-onset diabetes) results from the body's ineffective use of insulin. More than 95% of people with diabetes have type 2 diabetes. This type of diabetes is increasingly being diagnosed in more children and adolescents.

### Gestational Diabetes
Gestational diabetes is hyperglycemia with first recognition during pregnancy. The main symptoms of gestational diabetes are increased urination, increased thirst, and fatigue.

## Symptoms of Diabetes

- Increased thirst (polydipsia)
- Increased urination (polyuria)
- Increased hunger (polyphagia)
- Unexplained weight loss
- Fatigue
- Blurred vision
- Slow-healing sores
- Frequent infections
- Areas of darkened skin
- Numbness or tingling in hands or feet

## Risk Factors for Type 2 Diabetes

- Being overweight or obese
- Being 45 or older
- Having a parent or sibling with type 2 diabetes
- Being physically active less than 3 times a week
- Having prediabetes
- Having had gestational diabetes during pregnancy
- Having polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS)

## Management

### Diet
A balanced diet is essential for managing diabetes. Key dietary recommendations include:
- Monitoring carbohydrate intake and choosing complex carbohydrates
- Eating plenty of fiber (vegetables, fruits, whole grains, legumes)
- Choosing healthy fats (olive oil, nuts, avocados)
- Limiting processed foods and added sugars
- Eating regular meals and not skipping breakfast
- Controlling portion sizes

### Exercise
Regular physical activity helps control blood sugar levels and improves insulin sensitivity. Recommendations:
- At least 150 minutes of moderate aerobic activity per week
- Strength training exercises 2 or more days per week
- Reduce prolonged sitting by getting up every 30 minutes

### Blood Sugar Monitoring
Regular monitoring of blood glucose levels is essential for diabetes management. The American Diabetes Association recommends:
- Fasting blood glucose target: 80-130 mg/dL
- Post-meal blood glucose target: less than 180 mg/dL
- HbA1c target: less than 7%

### Medication
Treatment may include oral medications (metformin, sulfonylureas, etc.) and/or insulin therapy, depending on the type and severity of diabetes.

## Complications of Uncontrolled Diabetes

- Cardiovascular disease (heart attack, stroke)
- Neuropathy (nerve damage)
- Nephropathy (kidney damage)
- Retinopathy (eye damage, potential blindness)
- Foot damage (risk of amputation)
- Skin conditions
- Hearing impairment
- Alzheimer's disease
