# First Aid Basics

## Emergency Response Principles

### The Chain of Survival
1. Recognize the emergency and call for help
2. Early access to emergency medical services
3. Early CPR with emphasis on chest compressions
4. Early defibrillation (if available)
5. Early advanced care

### When to Call Emergency Services
- Difficulty breathing or shortness of breath
- Chest pain or pressure
- Severe bleeding
- Loss of consciousness
- Suspected stroke (face drooping, arm weakness, speech difficulty)
- Severe allergic reaction (anaphylaxis)
- Seizures
- Severe burns
- Head, neck, or spinal injuries
- Drowning
- Poisoning

## CPR (Cardiopulmonary Resuscitation)

### Hands-Only CPR for Adults
1. Check if the person is responsive
2. Call 911 or ask someone to call
3. Push hard and fast in the center of the chest
4. Rate: 100-120 compressions per minute
5. Depth: at least 2 inches (5 cm)
6. Allow full chest recoil between compressions
7. Continue until emergency services arrive

### CPR with Rescue Breaths
After 30 chest compressions, give 2 rescue breaths:
1. Tilt the head back, lift the chin
2. Pinch the nose shut
3. Create a complete seal over the mouth
4. Give 2 breaths, each lasting 1 second
5. Continue 30:2 ratio

## Choking Response

### Conscious Adult/Child
1. Ask "Are you choking?"
2. If the person cannot speak, cough, or breathe:
   - Stand behind the person
   - Make a fist with one hand
   - Place the fist above the navel
   - Grasp the fist with the other hand
   - Perform quick upward thrusts
3. Continue until the object is expelled or the person becomes unconscious

### Unconscious Adult
1. Call 911
2. Begin CPR
3. Look for the object in the mouth before giving breaths
4. If visible, remove it
5. Continue CPR

## Bleeding Control

### Direct Pressure
1. Apply firm, direct pressure with a clean cloth or gauze
2. Maintain pressure for at least 15 minutes
3. Add more layers if blood soaks through
4. Do not remove the first layer of cloth

### Severe Bleeding
1. Call 911 immediately
2. Apply firm pressure
3. If bleeding is from an extremity, consider a tourniquet
4. Keep the person warm and calm
5. Do not remove the object causing the wound

## Burns

### First-Degree Burns
- Affects only the outer layer of skin
- Treatment: Cool water for 10 minutes, aloe vera, OTC pain relievers

### Second-Degree Burns
- Affects outer and underlying layers
- Treatment: Cool water, do not pop blisters, seek medical attention

### Third-Degree Burns
- Affects all layers of skin
- Treatment: Call 911, do not remove clothing, cover with clean cloth, do not apply water

## Allergic Reactions (Anaphylaxis)

### Symptoms
- Swelling of face, lips, throat
- Difficulty breathing
- Hives or rash
- Rapid pulse
- Dizziness or fainting
- Nausea or vomiting

### Treatment
1. Use epinephrine auto-injector (EpiPen) if available
2. Call 911 immediately
3. Lay the person down, elevate legs
4. Give second dose after 5-15 minutes if no improvement
5. Monitor breathing and pulse

## Heat-Related Illnesses

### Heat Exhaustion
- Heavy sweating, weakness, cold/pale/clammy skin
- Move to cool place, loosen clothing, apply cool cloths
- Sip water slowly
- Seek medical attention if vomiting occurs

### Heat Stroke (Medical Emergency)
- High body temperature (103°F or higher), hot/red/dry skin
- Rapid pulse, confusion, unconsciousness
- Call 911 immediately
- Move to cooler environment
- Cool body rapidly with cold water or ice packs
- Do NOT give fluids if unconscious

## When NOT to Move Someone
- Suspected spinal injury
- Head, neck, or back pain
- Arm or leg numbness
- Fractures
- Loss of consciousness without airway problems
