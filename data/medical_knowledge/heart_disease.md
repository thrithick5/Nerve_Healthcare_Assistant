# Heart Disease - Overview

## What is Heart Disease?

Heart disease, also known as cardiovascular disease, refers to conditions that involve narrowed or blocked blood vessels that can lead to a heart attack, chest pain (angina), or stroke. It is the leading cause of death worldwide. Common heart diseases include coronary artery disease, heart failure, arrhythmias, and valve problems.

## Types of Heart Disease

### Coronary Artery Disease (CAD)
The most common type of heart disease. CAD occurs when the coronary arteries become narrowed or blocked due to plaque buildup (atherosclerosis). This reduces blood flow to the heart muscle and can lead to heart attack.

### Heart Failure
Heart failure means the heart cannot pump blood as well as it should. Despite the name, it does not mean the heart has stopped working. It means the heart is not pumping as well as the body needs.

### Arrhythmias
Arrhythmias are irregular heartbeats. The heart may beat too quickly (tachycardia), too slowly (bradycardia), or with an irregular pattern.

### Heart Valve Disease
When one or more heart valves do not work properly, the heart must work harder to pump blood through the body.

## Risk Factors

### Non-Modifiable Risk Factors
- Age (men over 45, women over 55)
- Gender (men at higher risk at younger ages)
- Family history of heart disease
- Race/ethnicity (higher risk for certain populations)

### Modifiable Risk Factors
- High blood pressure (hypertension)
- High cholesterol
- Diabetes
- Obesity (BMI > 30)
- Smoking
- Physical inactivity
- Unhealthy diet
- Excessive alcohol consumption
- Chronic stress

## Warning Signs

### Heart Attack Symptoms
- Chest pain or discomfort (pressure, squeezing, fullness)
- Pain in one or both arms, the back, neck, jaw, or stomach
- Shortness of breath
- Cold sweat, nausea, lightheadedness
- Women may experience atypical symptoms: unusual fatigue, indigestion-like discomfort

### Heart Failure Symptoms
- Shortness of breath during activity or when lying down
- Fatigue and weakness
- Swelling in legs, ankles, and feet
- Rapid or irregular heartbeat
- Reduced ability to exercise
- Persistent cough or wheezing
- Swelling of abdomen
- Rapid weight gain from fluid retention

## Diagnosis

- Electrocardiogram (ECG/EKG)
- Echocardiogram
- Stress test
- Cardiac catheterization
- Blood tests (troponin, BNP, cholesterol)
- Coronary angiogram
- CT scan or MRI

## Prevention

### Diet
- Heart-healthy diet (DASH or Mediterranean)
- Limit saturated fat to less than 10% of calories
- Limit sodium to less than 2,300 mg/day
- Eat plenty of fruits, vegetables, whole grains
- Choose lean proteins
- Include omega-3 rich foods

### Exercise
- At least 150 minutes of moderate aerobic activity per week
- Or 75 minutes of vigorous aerobic activity per week
- Muscle-strengthening activities 2+ days per week

### Lifestyle
- Don't smoke or quit smoking
- Maintain healthy weight (BMI 18.5-24.9)
- Manage stress
- Get adequate sleep (7-9 hours)
- Limit alcohol consumption
- Manage diabetes, high blood pressure, and cholesterol

## Treatment

### Medications
- Statins (lower cholesterol)
- ACE inhibitors or ARBs (lower blood pressure)
- Beta-blockers (reduce heart workload)
- Antiplatelet agents (prevent blood clots)
- Diuretics (reduce fluid buildup)
- Blood thinners (prevent clots)

### Surgical Procedures
- Angioplasty and stent placement
- Coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG)
- Heart valve repair or replacement
- Heart transplant (in severe cases)

## When to Seek Emergency Care

Call emergency services immediately if you experience:
- Chest pain lasting more than a few minutes
- Severe shortness of breath
- Fainting or loss of consciousness
- Rapid or irregular heartbeat with dizziness
- Sudden severe weakness or numbness
- Sudden difficulty speaking
